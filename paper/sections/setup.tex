\label{sec:setup}

Protocol decisions are listed here in full because several of them changed a conclusion's
sign, and \S\ref{sec:protocol} reports by how much.

\paragraph{Matched encoder.} Every method under comparison---ours, every baseline, every
ablation---is handed identical vectors from the same frozen model. Text uses
\texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2} \citep{reimers2019sbert}, $d{=}768$; images use ImageNet-pretrained
ResNet-18 features \citep{he2016resnet}, $d{=}512$. Encoders are never fine-tuned, and no
result in this paper can be attributed to representation quality.

\paragraph{Corpora.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Images (completion regime).} $400$ Imagenette images ($40$ per class, ten
  classes) from the \texttt{train} split as the memory bank; queries are occluded images
  from the \texttt{val} split at $30/50/70\%$ occlusion. Evaluation uses $5{,}847$ query
  instances drawn from $1{,}949$ distinct images, the full validation split. \textbf{Two
  properties of this design are load-bearing and were missing from earlier versions of this
  paper.} The splits are disjoint, so a cue's own source image is \emph{not} in the bank;
  and the metric is \textbf{same-class} top-1 accuracy --- a retrieval is correct when the
  returned memory shares the query's class --- so the task is ten-way classification through
  a retrieval interface, with chance at $0.100$. \S\ref{sec:occlusion} reports what follows
  from both facts, and \xid{X113b} supplies the same-image variant in which the cue is an
  occluded copy of a pattern that \emph{is} stored.
\item \textbf{\textsc{LoCoMo} (semantic-match regime).} $1{,}977$ real questions over
  $5{,}882$ conversational turns \citep{maharana2024locomo}. Metric: Coverage@5 over gold
  evidence turns.
\item \textbf{\textsc{LoCoMo} (composition regime).} The same $5{,}882$ turns, re-used as
  temporal-association probes: cue a turn, retrieve the turn that followed it in the same
  session. $5{,}233$ probes at one hop and $5{,}013$ at two, over $272$ sessions. The
  candidate set is the full pooled bank of all ten conversations, with dialogue identifiers
  namespaced by conversation (Appendix~\ref{app:locomo}). This is the external-validity
  evidence for \S\ref{sec:composition}, and it is a corpus we did not build.
\item \textbf{Episodic corpus (composition regime).} A purpose-built store of $1{,}319$
  episodes across $122$ storylines with $146$ multi-target questions, plus $900$
  compositional filler episodes and planted near-miss distractors. Association probes are
  generated from it as consecutive (or $h$-distant) within-storyline pairs: $257$ probes at
  one hop and $135$ at two. This corpus is ours, it is generated rather than collected, and
  on its own it would not support a claim; every composition result measured on it is
  reported beside the \textsc{LoCoMo} measurement of the same quantity.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Baselines.} Dense top-$k$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}), BM25
\citep{robertson2009bm25}, hybrid reciprocal-rank fusion \citep{cormack2009rrf}, and---in the
composition regime---a parametric JEPA predictor. All lexical and hybrid baselines receive
the same tuning budget as our methods, stated per experiment in Appendix~\ref{app:grids}.

\paragraph{Splits and selection.} Hyperparameters are never selected on the data a number is
reported on. In the composition regime the split unit is the \emph{storyline} on our corpus
and the \emph{session} on \textsc{LoCoMo}, never the question: a model fitted on transitions
from one storyline or session is never evaluated on it. Because the corpus holds only $122$ storylines, a single $50/25/25$ split leaves
$61$ one-hop and $30$ two-hop test probes, intervals on which span most of the plausible
range before the experiment begins. We therefore use thread-level $4$-fold rotation: every
storyline is in the test set exactly once and is never in the train or validation fold that
tests it. Both the world model's own $(k,\tau)$ and the readout's parameters are selected
per fold on that fold's validation storylines.

\paragraph{Intervals.} Headline numbers are \emph{paired} differences against the baseline on
the same queries, with $95\%$ bootstrap intervals ($20{,}000$ resamples) over the
\emph{cluster}---questions, storylines, or source images as appropriate---never over
individual measurements. Comparing two overlapping marginal intervals is the wrong test when
both methods see the same queries; resampling rows rather than clusters is pseudo-replication
and we quantify what it cost us in \S\ref{sec:protocol}. Image-modality intervals resample
\emph{images}, not query instances, since instances from one image are correlated across
occlusion levels and seeds.

\paragraph{Dimensionless hyperparameters.} Every scale that crosses dimensionalities is
normalised before use: a perturbation of nominal magnitude $\sigma$ is applied as
$\sigma/\sqrt{d}$, so that its displacement norm is dimension-independent. At $d{=}768$ the
unnormalised form is wrong by a factor of $28$.

\paragraph{Symmetric candidate sets.} Where a cue is itself a stored memory---as in every
association probe---it is excluded from the candidate set for \emph{every} method
identically, before ranking. Asymmetry here produces exact zeros that read as findings.

\paragraph{Latency.} No accuracy number appears without its cost. Iterative descent at
$T{=}8$ costs $21\times$ cosine in measured wall-clock ($0.0502$\,ms vs.\ $0.0024$\,ms per
query), and multi-$\beta$ ensembling a further $1.6$--$2.9\times$. We report both wall-clock
and matmul counts, because they diverge: the integrated mixture issues the same $16$ matmuls
as single-$\beta$ descent and costs $1.6\times$ the wall-clock, since mixing $m$ softmaxes
per step is arithmetic outside the matmuls. Figures in Table~\ref{tab:multibeta} and
Appendix~\ref{app:grids}.

\paragraph{Record.} Every experiment is logged in an append-only ledger with an identifier,
the question it asked, the result, and what it changed. Entries are never edited after their
result is in; an overturned entry becomes a new entry pointing back at it. Identifiers of
the form \xid{X$n$} in this paper index that ledger, including the entries that overturn our
own earlier claims.
