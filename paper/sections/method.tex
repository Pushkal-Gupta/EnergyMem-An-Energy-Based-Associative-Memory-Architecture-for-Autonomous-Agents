\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and notation}

Memories $m_1,\dots,m_N$ are embedded by a frozen encoder $E$ into unit vectors
$z_i = E(m_i) \in \mathcal{S}^{d-1}$ and stacked as $X = [z_1 \cdots z_N] \in \R^{d\times N}$.
A cue $x$ is embedded to $q = E(x)$, and the retrieval state is initialised at
$\xib^{(0)} = q$. Write $\Pi[\cdot]$ for projection back to the sphere, $b \in \R^N$ for a
per-memory strength bias, $\beta>0$ for an inverse temperature, and $\lse(\cdot)$ for the
log-sum-exp. The modern Hopfield energy \citep{ramsauer2021hopfield} is
\begin{equation}
E_M(\xib) \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{\beta}\lse\!\big(\beta(X^{\!\top}\xib + b)\big) \;+\; \tfrac12\|\xib\|^2,
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
minimised by the concave--convex (CCCP) update, which we run for $T$ steps:
\begin{equation}
\xib^{(t+1)} \;=\; \Pi\!\Big[(1-\eta)\,X\,\sm\!\big(\beta(X^{\!\top}\xib^{(t)}+b)\big) \;+\; \eta\, R\big(\xib^{(t)}\big)\Big].
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:update} decreases \eqref{eq:energy} monotonically and converges for
$\eta=0$. The operator $R$ is a \emph{routing} map, discussed in \S\ref{sec:axes}.

\subsection{Dense retrieval is a corner of this family}

\begin{proposition}[The reduction]
\label{prop:reduction}
Let the patterns $z_i$ and the cue $q$ be $L_2$-normalised, and set $T=1$, $b=0$, $\eta=0$.
Then as $\beta\to\infty$ the state after one update converges to the stored pattern of
maximal cosine similarity to the cue,
\[
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\ \Pi\!\big[X\,\sm(\beta X^{\!\top} q)\big] \;=\; z_{i^\star},
\qquad i^\star = \arg\max_i\, \langle z_i, q\rangle ,
\]
whenever the maximiser is unique. The reduction is therefore \emph{exact at $k=1$}. For
$k>1$ the dense top-$k$ neighbourhood is recovered by reading the ranking off the
\emph{cue}, $\langle z_i,q\rangle$ --- the standard definition, and the one our dense
baseline uses --- and \emph{not} off the converged state: ranks $2..k$ of
$\langle z_i,\xib^{(1)}\rangle$ order the patterns by proximity to $z_{i^\star}$, not to
$q$, because the state has forgotten the cue by construction. Remark~\ref{rem:shortlist}
makes this precise, and \S\ref{sec:completion} shows it is the mechanism behind the
shortlist regression rather than a technicality.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
With $b=0$ the logits are $s_i = \langle z_i,q\rangle$. Let $\delta = s_{i^\star} - \max_{i\neq i^\star} s_i > 0$.
Then $\sm(\beta s)_{i^\star} = \big(1 + \sum_{i\neq i^\star} e^{-\beta(s_{i^\star}-s_i)}\big)^{-1}
\geq (1 + (N-1)e^{-\beta\delta})^{-1} \to 1$, and every other coordinate is
$O(e^{-\beta\delta})$. Hence $X\sm(\beta X^{\!\top}q) \to z_{i^\star}$, and normalisation fixes
the point since $\|z_{i^\star}\|=1$. The full argument --- with the explicit
$O\big((N{-}1)e^{-\beta\delta}\big)$ rate, the tied-maximiser case, and the analysis of
what happens for $k>1$ --- is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.
\end{proof}

We treat Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} as an executable claim rather than a remark. The
test suite instantiates the configuration above and asserts \emph{top-1} agreement with an
independent exact-cosine implementation on every query --- the quantity the proposition
states, no more; it passes $1000/1000$ on synthetic vectors
($d{=}64$) and $838/838$ on real sentence embeddings ($d{=}768$), to floating-point
precision, and it gates continuous integration. If it ever fails, no downstream number in this paper is trustworthy
(\xid{X1}).

Two consequences organise everything that follows.

\emph{First, the baseline cannot be disadvantaged.} ``Dense top-$k$'' in every table below
is Equation~\eqref{eq:update} with the arguments of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, run
through the same code path, the same encoder and the same candidate set as the method under
test. The most common rejection reason for papers of this shape---that the authors tuned
their own method and not the baseline---is unavailable here by construction.

\emph{Second, the research question becomes finite.} The reduction identifies a specific
point in a parameter space; the scientific content is which coordinates are worth moving.

\subsection{Three axes away from the corner}
\label{sec:axes}

Exactly three coordinates of Equation~\eqref{eq:update} are free, and each encodes a
distinct hypothesis about what agent memory needs.

\begin{description}\itemsep3pt
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{G1} --- depth, $T>1$.}] Iterating repairs a degraded cue.
  Cosine cannot imitate this: it has no mechanism for revisiting a cue.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{G2} --- basin depth, $b\neq 0$.}] A memory used constantly earns a
  deeper basin and therefore a wider one, so a vaguer cue still finds it. This folds usage,
  recency and surprise into a single term of the energy instead of three heuristics blended
  by hand outside it. Cosine can partially imitate it with an additive score bonus.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{G3} --- routing, $R\neq I$.}] Memories that co-occur in time pull
  on one another, so falling into one drags the state toward its companions. This is the only
  axis that can express a relation cosine structurally cannot represent, and it is the axis
  on which our one positive result lives. Critically, $R$ must be fitted from temporal
  co-occurrence and never from embedding similarity: fitting it on similarity collapses G3
  back into the baseline and silently invalidates the claim.
\end{description}

\subsection{The $\beta$ leverage band}
\label{sec:band}

Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} has a corollary that is easy to state and, we will argue,
widely consequential.

\begin{corollary}[High-$\beta$ degeneracy]
\label{cor:degeneracy}
For any $T$, as $\beta\to\infty$ with $b=0,\eta=0$ the trajectory of
Equation~\eqref{eq:update} is constant after the first step, because stored patterns are
exact fixed points. Multi-step descent at high $\beta$ is therefore \emph{identical} to
one-step descent, which by Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} is cosine retrieval.
\end{corollary}

Empirically the degeneracy arrives early, and accuracy is the wrong way to see it. Two
retrievers can score identically while disagreeing about which memory they return, so we
measure \emph{top-1 decision agreement} with cosine --- the fraction of queries on which
descent returns the exact memory cosine would have (Figure~\ref{fig:band},
\texttt{sweep\_beta\_transition.py}).

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{figures/beta_band.pdf}
\caption{The $\beta$ leverage band, on the held-out image task. \textbf{Left axis:}
same-class top-1 accuracy against the cosine baseline (dashed); the metric is ten-way
classification, which is why the collapse floor is $0.100 = 1/10$ (\S\ref{sec:occlusion}).
\textbf{Right axis:} the fraction of queries on which descent returns the \emph{same memory}
as cosine. Below the band the state collapses to the corpus centroid; above it descent makes
cosine's decision on $99.4\%$ of queries. The interval in between is where $\beta$ has any
influence at all --- which is not the same as where it is useful: \S\ref{sec:occlusion}
shows the interior optimum is beaten by a classifier.}
\label{fig:band}
\end{figure}

The curve has two failures and one working region. Below $\beta \approx 24$ the softmax is
flat enough that the first step averages a large fraction of the bank and the state lands at
its centroid: same-class accuracy is $0.100$ at $\beta{=}12$, which is exactly chance for a ten-class
bank, and decision agreement with cosine is $1\%$. Above the band, descent converges on
cosine's own answer --- agreement rises through $86\%$ at $\beta{=}64$ and $96\%$ at
$\beta{=}128$ to $\mathbf{99.4\%}$ at $\beta{=}512$, where accuracy is $0.4986$ against
cosine's $0.5000$. Between them is a genuine interior optimum: $\beta{=}48$ reaches $0.514$,
$+0.014$ over cosine, while still disagreeing with it on $27\%$ of queries. The band is
narrow --- roughly $[32, 64]$ on this encoder, with leverage over perhaps a factor of two in
$\beta$. We now read that interior optimum as the width at which the blend acts as a class
vote rather than as an attractor: at $\beta{=}40$ the first step has an effective support of
$12.7$ patterns out of $400$ (\xid{X113}). The band is where $\beta$ matters; it is not
where the method wins.

Three implications. (i) The apparent \emph{plateau} in accuracy above $\beta \approx 64$,
which a coarse sweep reads as a robust operating region, is the method dissolving into the
baseline rather than working; the agreement curve distinguishes the two and the accuracy
curve cannot. (ii) The band is a property of the encoder and must be re-measured per
encoder: the interval above is for ResNet-18 features ($d{=}512$), while on
\texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2} ($d{=}768$) the lower edge sits higher --- at $\beta{=}16$ the
effective number of patterns in the blend is $377$ (\xid{X11}), against a threshold of
$\approx 16$ on isotropic synthetic vectors (\xid{X3}). The threshold survives as a
mechanism and dies as a number. (iii) Any experiment comparing ``Hopfield retrieval''
against ``cosine retrieval'' at high $\beta$ is comparing cosine against cosine on nearly
every query, and will correctly report no difference for the wrong reason. We flag this as
a live hazard for this literature; we committed it ourselves, and \S\ref{sec:protocol}
reports what it cost us.

\subsection{Beyond the landscape: memory as a model of what follows what}
\label{sec:worldmodel}

Every mechanism in \S\ref{sec:axes} except G3 changes how an answer is \emph{read off} a
fixed landscape. None of them changes the landscape. The results in \S\ref{sec:negatives}
make that distinction empirically load-bearing: readout mechanisms uniformly fail on agent
text.

G3 is different in kind, and the formulation we adopt for it is the joint-embedding
predictive one \citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}: predict the \emph{representation} of
the next episode, not its tokens. An agent's episode stream is a trajectory, and temporal
adjacency is free supervision---no labelling is required to know which episode followed
which.

Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the set of observed consecutive index pairs, $Z_c$ the matrix of cue
representations over $\mathcal{T}$, and $\Delta_i = z_{\text{next}(i)} - z_{\text{cue}(i)}$
the displacement each transition took. We consider two instantiations.

\emph{Parametric.} A low-rank predictor trained with an InfoNCE objective against a frozen
target encoder. This is the natural first choice and it fails completely
(\S\ref{sec:composition}): it memorises the storylines it was fitted on.

\emph{Nonparametric.} Carry the transitions rather than compressing them, and transfer
displacements locally:
\begin{equation}
P(q) \;=\; \Pi\!\Big[q \;+\; \alpha \textstyle\sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}_k(q)} w_i\, \Delta_i\Big],
\qquad w = \sm\big(\tau\, Z_c^{\!\top} q\big)\big|_{\mathcal{N}_k(q)} ,
\label{eq:knnwm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{N}_k(q)$ are the $k$ nearest remembered cues and $\alpha$ is a step size.
This is still a world model---it predicts what comes next in embedding space---and it
composes: iterating it rolls the model forward. It is the one mechanism in this paper that
beats cosine on held-out data.

The step size $\alpha$ is not cosmetic, and \S\ref{sec:protocol} explains why we are
emphatic about it. A single remembered displacement has norm $1.157$ against a unit-norm
cue, so at $\alpha{=}1$ the prediction is dominated by the displacement and lands
essentially on $z_{\text{next}(i)}$---a \emph{stored training episode}, $100\%$ of the time
on our corpus against a bank prior of $14\%$. The kernel in \eqref{eq:knnwm} implicitly
applies $\alpha \approx 0.34$ through cancellation among near-orthogonal displacements, and
that shrinkage, not the weighting, is what keeps the cue in control of where the prediction
lands (\xid{X23b}). Any comparison between predictors that does not match effective step
size measures step size.
