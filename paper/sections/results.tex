Table~\ref{tab:images} was for most of this project the paper's one clean positive for the
energy mechanism itself, and the first three columns are unchanged from that version:
descent beats cosine by $+0.014$ pooled, growing with corruption severity, on a
pre-committed held-out split. The fourth column is why we no longer report it that way. A
soft $k$-NN class vote --- selected on the same tune half, from a grid declared in advance
--- scores $0.602$ where descent scores $0.566$, and beats descent at every occlusion
level. \S\ref{sec:occlusion} gives the mechanism: on a \emph{class} metric, descent at
$\beta{=}40$ is an implicit class vote over $\approx 13$ neighbours, and an explicit vote
over $k{=}20$ does the same job better. Table~\ref{tab:completion} then asks the question
the metric name implied all along --- can descent retrieve the \emph{particular} stored
image a cue was degraded from --- and the answer is $-0.003$ \ci{-0.008}{+0.000}.

The latency comparison is unchanged and now lands differently: descent costs $21\times$
cosine's wall-clock latency ($0.0502$\,ms vs.\ $0.0024$\,ms per query) to be beaten by a
classifier that costs one extra argpartition.

Two further qualifications, which predate \xid{X113} and survive it. First, the apparent
advantage is \emph{capacity-dependent}: growing the bank from $100$ to $4{,}000$ patterns at
fixed $\beta{=}32$, cosine overtakes descent past $N\approx 800$. Second, descent's rank-1
gain over cosine comes with a \emph{shortlist regression}: recall@5 is $0.605$ against cosine's $0.759$
and MRR $0.589$ against $0.643$, all class-level (Table~\ref{tab:multibeta}). Converging to a single
attractor means ranks 2--5 measure proximity to that attractor rather than to the query,
which is Remark~\ref{rem:shortlist} and is now confirmed in three independent settings.

\paragraph{Two earlier verdicts of ours, withdrawn.} A previous version of this study used
$80$ distinct images with unpaired intervals roughly $5\times$ the effect size and $\beta$
tuned on the test images; those conclusions were underpowered and Table~\ref{tab:images}
supersedes them. The version that replaced it --- the one with the honest split, which we
described as a clean positive for the energy mechanism --- is withdrawn in turn by its own
fourth column. The number stands; the interpretation does not.

\paragraph{A data bug that invalidated an earlier result.} \textsc{LoCoMo} dialogue

($0.250 \to 0.231$), merely less steeply than fixed $\beta$ ($0.244 \to 0.216$). We report
this because the failure mode is specific to this widely used benchmark and will recur.

\subsection{Composition: the one mechanism that works}
\label{sec:composition}

\paragraph{The gate.} Before building a predictor we checked whether cosine already solves
``what happened next''. It does not: Recall@1 is $0.105$ with the gold episode at median rank
$24$ of $1{,}319$. The failures are instructive---they are lexical collisions across
unrelated storylines, where a cue about a cottage drive returns ``forty minutes each way''
from an apartment-hunting thread. Oracle-restricting candidates to the correct storyline
still reaches only $0.488$, so the difficulty is not merely retrieval breadth. Temporal
succession is genuinely not a similarity relation (\xid{X16}).

\paragraph{The parametric predictor fails completely.} A low-rank JEPA predictor fitted on
some storylines and evaluated on others reaches Recall@1 of $1.000$ on the storylines it was
fitted on and $0.000$ on unseen ones, against the cosine baseline's $0.131$---worse
than doing nothing. No capacity escapes it: across ranks $2$ to $32$ the best unseen
Recall@1 at any setting is $0.016$, and the two largest capacities score exactly zero.
The diagnostic explains why: displacement vectors between consecutive episodes are
near-orthogonal (mean pairwise cosine $+0.004$) and the top ten principal
components carry only $31\%$ of their variance. There is no global ``and then''
direction to fit, and $134$ transitions in $768$ dimensions cannot teach one
(\xid{X17}, re-run as \xid{X17-r}). We report this as a result about inductive bias, not as
a tuning failure.

\paragraph{The nonparametric predictor works, on our corpus and on a public one.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:knnwm}, with $(k,\tau)$ selected on held-out storylines or sessions only,
beats cosine on one-hop association in four independent measurements over three corpora
(Table~\ref{tab:assoc}). The last two are the ones that matter: they run the same probe
design on \textsc{LoCoMo} and on \textsc{MSC}, benchmarks built by other groups. On
\textsc{LoCoMo} the run is against the \emph{full pooled bank} of
$5{,}882$ turns from all ten conversations, so a prediction must beat distractors from nine
unrelated conversations as well as its own. Dialogue identifiers are namespaced by
conversation---without that, $5{,}882$ turns carry only $1{,}033$ distinct identifiers and
distractors satisfy gold identifiers, the bug described in \S\ref{sec:semantic}.

On \textsc{LoCoMo} the effect is $+67\%$ relative at Recall@1 and $+0.077$
\ci{+0.067}{+0.087} at Recall@5 ($0.174 \to 0.251$).

\paragraph{It replicates on a second public corpus, five times the size.} Row (iv) of
Table~\ref{tab:assoc} runs the \emph{unmodified} \textsc{LoCoMo} driver on \textsc{MSC}
\citep{xu2022msc}: $27{,}815$ probes over $2{,}505$ sessions against a pooled bank of
$30{,}320$ turns, more than five times \textsc{LoCoMo}'s. Recall@1 rises from $0.049$ to
$0.065$, $\Delta = +0.016$ \ci{+0.014}{+0.018}, $p<10^{-4}$, $+33\%$ relative; Recall@5
gains $+0.028$ \ci{+0.026}{+0.031} (\xid{X201}). The absolute numbers are lower and the
relative effect is smaller than \textsc{LoCoMo}'s, which is what a bank five times larger
should do to a Recall@1. The direction, the significance and the mechanism are the same, on
a corpus we did not build, with code we did not change. This is the paper's load-bearing
positive claim and it is now the only one measured on two independent public corpora.

The mixture readout is again below plain averaging on \textsc{MSC} ($+0.006$ against
averaging's $+0.016$), reproducing the \textsc{LoCoMo} ordering of \S\ref{sec:modes} --- a
fourth corpus on which preserving the modes is worse than averaging them.

\paragraph{Multi-hop composition does not hold. We withdraw our own earlier result.}
An earlier run of ours indicated that iterating the predictor recovers two-hop chains it was
never trained on: Recall@5 of $0.446$ against cosine's $0.262$, $\Delta = +0.185$
\ci{+0.076}{+0.297}, $p<0.001$. That was the strongest result we had recorded. It does not
survive.

Three subsequent measurements (Table~\ref{tab:twohop}) return nulls, the last of them on
public data at $n{=}5{,}013$ with $272$ clusters---roughly $37\times$ the probe count of the
original. We therefore withdraw the two-hop claim as an artifact rather than suspending it.
The $+0.185$ figure should not be cited, including from our own ledger, where it now carries
a pointer to this result.

A residual signal does exist at two hops on \textsc{LoCoMo} at Recall@5 ($+0.012$
\ci{+0.004}{+0.019}), an order of magnitude below the one-hop effect and not visible at
Recall@1.

\paragraph{\textsc{MSC} makes the two-hop picture less clean, and we report it that way.}
Row (d) of Table~\ref{tab:twohop} is \emph{positive}: $\Delta$ Recall@1 $= +0.005$
\ci{+0.003}{+0.006} at $n{=}25{,}310$, with Recall@5 at $+0.010$ \ci{+0.008}{+0.012}. The
interval excludes zero, so this is not a null. It is also $3.4\times$ smaller than the same
corpus's one-hop effect and $9\times$ smaller than \textsc{LoCoMo}'s, and it sits at an
absolute Recall@1 of $0.017$. Our reading is that a second hop carries a real but very small
amount of signal that $25{,}310$ probes can resolve and $5{,}013$ cannot --- which is a
statement about power, not a rescue of the withdrawn claim. The withdrawn figure was
$+0.185$ at Recall@5; what survives is $+0.010$, a factor of $18$ below it.

We therefore state the summary with its qualification attached: association helps one step
ahead, and at two steps it either vanishes or shrinks to a margin too small to build on,
because the error introduced by predicting from a prediction grows faster than the
compositional signal. Any reader who wants the two-hop effect called positive on
\textsc{MSC} is entitled to; nothing in this paper rests on which way that is called.

\paragraph{Why we report this at length.} The withdrawn result was, for several weeks, the
headline of this project and the organising claim of a design document built on top of it.
It was overturned by reimplementation, not by review---twice---and the version that finally
settled it is the public-benchmark run that a reviewer would have demanded anyway. We regard
the sequence as the strongest available evidence for \S\ref{sec:protocol}'s argument that
independent reimplementation is a distinct and stronger property than peer inspection.

\subsection{Six more corpora, and the one departure that does pay}
\label{sec:corpora}

Everything to this point says the departures from the dense corner do not pay. One
pre-registered objection to that was still open, and it is the obvious one: every text
number above comes from \textsc{LoCoMo}. On \textsc{LoCoMo}, two-stage query expansion ---
retrieve $K_0$ modes, fold them into the cue at temperature $\beta$, retrieve again --- is
selected straight to its null, $K_0{=}0$. We could not tell whether that is a fact about the
mechanism or a fact about the corpus. The mean mutual cosine between a \textsc{LoCoMo}
question's gold evidence turns is $0.527$ --- the pieces of evidence already look like each
other, so a mode retrieved from one is close to what the cue would have found anyway. On a
corpus built so that the second piece of evidence is reachable only \emph{from the first},
expansion should have something to do.

\paragraph{Protocol.} \texttt{PREREG\_HI.md} was committed \emph{alone}, before any of these
corpora was downloaded, which is the fix for the earlier pre-registration whose file landed
in the same commit as its results (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). Six corpora go through the
unchanged harness: leave-one-group-out selection over ten groups on the training side only,
per-fold picks recorded in the \textsc{json}, cluster bootstrap over banks. One deviation is
logged in the pre-registration: the robustness checks below were chosen \emph{after} seeing
the first result, so they are exploratory and labelled as such.

\paragraph{The result.} Table~\ref{tab:corpora}. On chain-shaped questions, expansion wins,
and this is the first time in this project that a departure from the corner has: $+0.023$
\ci{+0.010}{+0.037} on \textsc{HotpotQA} bridge questions, $+0.171$ \ci{+0.118}{+0.219} on
\textsc{2Wiki} inference and $+0.067$ \ci{+0.042}{+0.091} on \textsc{2Wiki} compositional.
On comparison questions --- where both documents are reachable from the question directly
--- selection chooses $K_0{=}0$ in $10$ of $10$ folds on both corpora, and the method
correctly does nothing. On \textsc{QASPER}, whose banks are single papers of ${\approx}47$
paragraphs, expansion actively hurts the multi-evidence stratum, $-0.027$
\ci{-0.049}{-0.010}: with a bank that small the top-$K_0$ modes are mostly redundant
neighbours of each other, so expansion adds no information and costs precision.

\paragraph{Without the label, the gain is smaller and still there.} Every stratified number
above is conditional on knowing a question's shape, and that label comes from the benchmark,
not from a detector we built (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}). The obvious objection is that the result
is therefore an oracle result, so we report the run that answers it: the same driver also
selects and scores on the \emph{unstratified} pool, where no label is available at any
stage --- one configuration chosen on a mixed training stream and applied to a mixed
evaluation stream. The gain survives on both multi-hop corpora. \textsc{HotpotQA} gives
$+0.015$ \ci{+0.004}{+0.025} over $n{=}2{,}000$, against $+0.023$ on the bridge stratum that
is $80\%$ of it; \textsc{2Wiki} gives $+0.043$ \ci{+0.029}{+0.056} over $n{=}2{,}000$,
against a chain fraction of $54\%$. In both cases selection declines the null in $0/10$
folds, so it is acting on the pooled stream rather than backing off from it, and in both
cases the unstratified effect is close to the stratum-weighted average of the parts --- the
label changes what we can say in advance about \emph{which} queries pay, not whether the
method pays. The corpora with no chain stratum behave as that reading requires: unstratified
\textsc{QASPER} is $+0.023$ \ci{-0.002}{+0.047} and \textsc{NFCorpus} $+0.009$
\ci{-0.004}{+0.021}, both spanning zero, and \textsc{LongMemEval} is the $-0.007$ already in
the table. We take the unstratified rows, not the stratified ones, to be the honest size of
this result for anyone who wants to use it.

\paragraph{The hypothesis this kills is ours.} We had pre-registered that the gain should
grow with the number of targets $m$. Table~\ref{tab:cardinality} collects every test of
that prediction. It is \emph{reversed} on four corpora, confirmed on one, null on two. On
\textsc{2Wiki} the comparison is internal and therefore the cleanest: $m{=}4$ questions gain
$-0.023$ \ci{-0.044}{-0.004} \emph{less} than $m{=}2$ questions, and the $m{=}4$ stratum is
a flat $-0.001$. What predicts a gain is hop type, not cardinality. We withdraw the
cardinality prediction rather than reporting it as unsupported, because a prediction that
comes out backwards on four of seven tests has been refuted, not merely unconfirmed.

\paragraph{Three checks, and the one that carries the weight.} The expansion gain survives a
disjoint question sample, a second encoder (\texttt{bge}), and banks at $\tfrac{1}{2}\times$
and $2\times$: \textsc{HotpotQA} bridge stays within $+0.020$ to $+0.026$ in all four
variants, \textsc{2Wiki} inference within $+0.098$ to $+0.185$. The check that matters more
is the control: replace the retrieved modes with \emph{random} bank vectors at the same
$K_0$ and $\beta$, and the real modes win by $+0.13$ to $+0.39$ on \textsc{HotpotQA} and
$+0.16$ to $+0.36$ on \textsc{2Wiki}, in $9$ of $9$ cells each. The gain is therefore in
\emph{which} memories are folded into the cue, not in the act of folding something into it
--- which is the distinction that makes this an associative effect rather than a query-length
artefact, and it is the one control we would have wanted a reviewer to demand.

One sub-claim does \emph{not} survive. Whether the modes are kept apart (logsumexp) or
averaged is encoder-dependent: the advantage of keeping them apart is $+0.009$
\ci{+0.002}{+0.016} on \textsc{HotpotQA} under \texttt{mpnet} and $+0.001$
\ci{-0.004}{+0.006} under \texttt{bge}. The robust finding is expansion from retrieved
content; the combiner is a second-order detail, and \S\ref{sec:modes} should be read with
that in mind.

\paragraph{It does not transfer, which bounds the claim hard.} Configurations selected on
\textsc{LoCoMo} transfer to these corpora only in the trivial sense that \textsc{LoCoMo}
selects $K_0{=}0$: carrying that choice over gives up the entire in-corpus gain
($-0.023$ on \textsc{HotpotQA} bridge, $-0.171$ on \textsc{2Wiki} inference). RRF's single
parameter, by contrast, transfers within $0.012$ everywhere. So this is not a default anyone
could ship; it is a mechanism that must be tuned on the corpus it will run on, and whose
benefit is confined to one question shape. A system paper would have to detect that shape
before retrieval, label-free, and we have not shown that is possible.

\paragraph{And a lexical baseline is still the thing to beat.} Reciprocal-rank fusion is
higher than expansion in absolute terms on \textsc{HotpotQA} ($-0.017$ \ci{-0.030}{-0.005}
pooled), on \textsc{QASPER} ($-0.044$) and overwhelmingly on \textsc{LongMemEval}
($-0.163$ \ci{-0.195}{-0.131}). Expansion beats it on \textsc{2Wiki} inference ($+0.093$
\ci{+0.045}{+0.140}) and compositional ($+0.061$), and the sign on \textsc{HotpotQA} flips
with the encoder ($+0.021$ under \texttt{bge}). The honest ranking is that the two methods
win in different places and that neither dominates, which is a weaker and more useful claim
than either arm alone would support.

\paragraph{What this does to the paper's thesis.} It narrows it, in the direction of being
true. The claim ``no departure from the dense corner pays for itself'' was the shape of this
paper for most of its life, and it is false: one departure pays, significantly, on a question
shape we can name in advance, with a random-mode control behind it and a mechanism that
makes sense. Three qualifications keep it from rescuing the rest. It is \emph{query
expansion} --- $T{=}1$ with the cue folded back in --- and not attractor dynamics, so it is
not evidence for the energy machinery that motivated the project; it does not transfer
across corpora; and on conversational memory, the setting this paper is actually about, it is
null, where a lexical hybrid gains $+0.156$. The departure that pays is the one that uses
the memory bank to \emph{expand a query}, not the one that uses it to \emph{complete a cue}.

\paragraph{And the second conversational corpus confirms the first.}
\textsc{LongMemEval-S} was the obvious replication target for \textsc{LoCoMo}'s negative,
and it reproduces it more strongly: expansion is $-0.007$ \ci{-0.032}{+0.018} overall,
$-0.049$ \ci{-0.083}{-0.014} on knowledge-update questions, and the pre-registered
multi-session interaction is $+0.002$ \ci{-0.040}{+0.045}. RRF gains $+0.156$
\ci{+0.125}{+0.189} overall and $+0.255$ on temporal-reasoning questions. Chat memory wants
dates, names and exact phrases; association adds nothing on top of them. Two independent
conversational corpora now say the same thing, which is as close to a settled negative as
this paper gets.

Two findings survive from the controls in Table~\ref{tab:multibeta}. The integrated
$[1,5,20]$ control collapsed to exact chance ($0.108 = 1/10$) with \emph{identical} accuracy
at every occlusion level: one bad scale inside a shared trajectory poisons the entire state.
The ensemble version of the same scales reaches $0.282$, because there a bad scale only
dilutes one vote among independent runs. This is direct evidence that the coupling between
scales is real and can hurt as easily as it can fail to help.

\subsection{The multi-modality result, and why it does not help}
\label{sec:modes}

The predictor of Equation~\eqref{eq:knnwm} returns a weighted average. A natural hypothesis,
and one we committed to in a design document before testing it, is that this is a mistake:
if a cue has several legitimate successors, their average is a point in embedding space where
nothing is stored---a ridge between basins rather than a basin. Preserving the modes should
therefore help, and should motivate a stochastic (Boltzmann) rather than deterministic
relaxation \citep{ackley1985boltzmann,welling2011sgld}.

The premise is correct. Among the top three modes of $P(\cdot\mid q)$ the mean mutual cosine
is $0.353$, and $97\%$ of cues have modes that mutually disagree (\xid{X24}). There is
genuinely a distribution for averaging to destroy.

The conclusion is wrong. With step size matched (\S\ref{sec:method}), the mixture readout
\[
\mathrm{score}(m) \;=\; \lse_s\!\big[\beta\langle \xib_s, z_m\rangle + \log w_s\big]
\]
\emph{loses} to the averaged predictor at one hop, $\Delta = -0.062$ \ci{-0.094}{-0.034}, and
ties at two (\xid{X25}). The off-manifold gap the hypothesis rested on is also much smaller than assumed:
mean nearest-neighbour cosine is $0.482$ for a real stored episode against $0.462$ for an
averaged prediction, a gap of $-0.021$.

The mechanism is the step size. Averaging does two things at once, and the one that matters
is not the one the hypothesis named: it shrinks the displacement by a factor of $0.34$
through cancellation among near-orthogonal transitions, and that shrinkage keeps the cue in
control of where the prediction lands. Preserving the modes removes the shrinkage. On this
data, averaging is not a failure to be fixed---it is what makes the predictor work.

\paragraph{A later measurement sharpens this, and the invariant turns out to be about the
wrong comparison.} The step-size account above predicts that where there is no displacement
to shrink, the result should flip. We tested that in the modality where descent appeared to
win, with a
cue that is a degraded copy of two stored patterns at once (the spliced images of
\S\ref{sec:occlusion}) and hypotheses that are the cue plus stored vectors rather than
displacements, so no step size is in play. It flips, decisively: logsumexp beats averaging
in $20$ of $21$ $(K_0,\beta)$ cells with intervals excluding zero, by $+0.178$
\ci{+0.152}{+0.205} at the cell selected on a disjoint half (\xid{X111}). The gap widens
with $\beta$ because the averaged combiner collapses onto its heaviest mode and throws the
cue away, while logsumexp degrades gracefully towards the cue.

So I4's \emph{stated} comparison --- keep the modes rather than average them --- is right,
and in the clean setting it is right by a wide margin. It is also irrelevant here: in the
same $21$ cells, \textbf{neither combiner ever beats using the cue alone} ($0/21$). The
invariant was never the binding constraint in this experiment. Phrased as ``never average
modes'' it is confirmed; phrased as a reason to build a multi-mode readout it is not,
because the comparison that decides whether to build one is against the single-query
baseline.

\paragraph{That last sentence had an exception, and we found it after writing it.} An
earlier draft of this subsection said the comparison against the single-query baseline
``has never once come out in favour''. \S\ref{sec:corpora} is where it does: on
chain-shaped multi-hop text the expanded cue beats the plain cue by up to $+0.171$, with
selection choosing a non-null $K_0$ in $10$ of $10$ folds. The two findings are compatible
and the boundary between them is informative. Here the hypotheses are the cue plus stored
image vectors and the targets are the mosaic's own sources, so expansion can only dilute a
cue that already contains everything needed. There the second document is not in the cue at
all and can only be reached through the first, so expansion supplies information the cue
does not have. What decides it is whether the retrieved modes carry anything the cue is
missing --- and the corpus, not the combiner, settles that.

We report this at length because it is the clearest instance in the paper of a correct
premise licensing a wrong design, because the first version of the experiment was
confounded by our own implementation in exactly the way \S\ref{sec:protocol} describes, and
because the invariant then turned out to be true in a form that changes nothing.
