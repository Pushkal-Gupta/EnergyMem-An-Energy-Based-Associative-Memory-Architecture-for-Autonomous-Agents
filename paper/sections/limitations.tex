\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Two public benchmarks, one task shape.} The one-hop positive replicates on
\textsc{LoCoMo} \citep{maharana2024locomo} and on \textsc{MSC} \citep{xu2022msc} at a bank
five times the size (\S\ref{sec:composition}), which discharges the external-validity concern
for that result about as well as two corpora can. It does not discharge it generally, and
the reason is the \emph{probe}, not the corpus. Cue a turn, retrieve the turn that followed
is one operationalisation of temporal association, and both corpora are
speaker-alternating dialogue, so both give next-turn prediction a regularity that a single
agent's episode stream may not share. Replicating on a second corpus of the same shape
cannot detect a bias that belongs to the shape. What this claim still lacks is a corpus
where the stream is one agent's own actions, and we know of no public one.

\paragraph{Association barely composes, and we do not know why.} Two hops is null on the
episodic corpus and on \textsc{LoCoMo}, and positive but $18\times$ smaller than the
withdrawn claim on \textsc{MSC} (\S\ref{sec:composition}). We attribute it to compounding prediction error
exceeding the compositional signal, but that is an interpretation rather than a measurement:
we have not isolated the error accumulated per step, and the small residual Recall@5 effect
on \textsc{LoCoMo} is unexplained. A rollout-depth sweep with per-step error attribution is
the experiment this calls for.

\paragraph{The retrieval task is not the downstream task, and the gap is now measured.}
Every number here is retrieval quality. We have since built the cheapest honest proxy for
the downstream question --- normalised substring coverage of the gold answer inside the
concatenated top-$k$ turns, on \textsc{LoCoMo}'s own annotated answers, deterministic and
API-free --- and \textbf{a large retrieval gain does not survive into it} (\xid{X35}). A
reciprocal-rank-fusion baseline that beats dense cosine by $+0.124$ on evidence
$\mathrm{recall}@10$ carries that into $+0.007$ \ci{-0.013}{+0.027} of answer coverage at
$k{=}10$: indistinguishable from zero. Coverage is a weak proxy and we call it coverage
rather than accuracy for that reason, but it moves in the direction that should be easiest
to move, and it did not. Whether \emph{any} of these retrieval differences change what an
agent does remains untested, and this result makes us less optimistic rather than more.

\paragraph{Our pseudo-relevance-feedback baseline never helps, anywhere, and we cannot yet
say why.} Rocchio feedback is the prior art a reader names first, and in our hands it is
selected straight to its null ($\gamma{=}0$) on every corpus and loses whenever it is forced
to do something. We therefore ran it on \textsc{SciFact}, a standard ad-hoc retrieval
benchmark where feedback methods are well calibrated: $0$ of $16$ swept cells beat cosine
there either (\xid{X104}). Two readings remain open --- that a strong dense baseline leaves
classical PRF nothing to add, which the literature on dense feedback would support, or that
our PRF is simply weak. Until that is settled, we do not claim ``the prior art does not help
either'', and any comparison in this paper against Rocchio should be read with that
reservation.

\paragraph{Scale.} Banks run from $47$ patterns (\textsc{QASPER}, one paper) to $30{,}320$
(\textsc{MSC}), with encoders of $512$, $768$ and --- for the cross-modal arm --- CLIP's
joint space. Both ends of that range matter and both are far from the target: the
$47$-paragraph banks are where expansion inverts and starts to hurt, and the
$30{,}320$-turn bank is still two orders of magnitude short of what a long-lived agent
accumulates. The image advantage is explicitly capacity-dependent---cosine overtakes
descent past $N\approx 800$ at fixed $\beta$---so nothing here licenses a claim at the
$10^6$-memory scale a long-lived agent would reach. Whether the crossover is a $\beta$-scaling
artefact is unresolved. It was the most important unanswered question about the completion
result until \xid{X113} removed the result; we retain it because the capacity behaviour of
descent is of independent interest.

\paragraph{The latency cost is real, and larger than the matmul count suggests.} Descent
at $T{=}8$ issues $16$ matmuls against cosine's $1$, but measured wall-clock is
$21\times$ ($0.0502$\,ms vs.\ $0.0024$\,ms per query), for a pooled $+0.014$ same-class
accuracy that a $k$-NN class vote exceeds at a fraction of the cost (\S\ref{sec:occlusion}).
Multi-$\beta$ variants cost a further $1.6$--$2.9\times$ for an effect indistinguishable
from zero. We do not think this trade is attractive in deployment and say so.

\paragraph{The shortlist regression.} Descent improves rank 1 and degrades ranks 2--5
($0.605$ vs.\ $0.759$ Recall@5). For an agent that pastes five memories into context, which
is the standard usage, this is plausibly a net loss.

\paragraph{The image study was not a completion study, and we did not notice for most of
this project.} Its metric was same-class accuracy and its cues were drawn from a split
disjoint from the bank, so the premise it was offered as confirming was never satisfied by
it (\S\ref{sec:occlusion}, \xid{X113}). We report this as a limitation of our own process,
not only of the result: the experiment had a pre-committed split, a declared grid and a
paired bootstrap, and none of that machinery can detect a baseline that was never allowed
to do its best, or a metric whose name does not match the operation it scores. Readers
weighing the rest of this paper should know that the defect was found by re-reading our
scoring code, which is the one audit we had not systematised.

\paragraph{The one positive departure is not deployable, and we say so rather than rounding
it up.} Expansion on chain-shaped multi-hop questions (\S\ref{sec:corpora}) is the only
mechanism in this paper that beats the dense corner on its own terms. Three things bound it.
It requires tuning on the corpus it will serve --- configurations selected on
\textsc{LoCoMo} give up the entire gain, where RRF's single parameter transfers within
$0.012$. It requires knowing the question's shape in advance, and we have not shown a
label-free signal that separates a chain question from a comparison question before
retrieval; the gain and the harm live in different strata of the \emph{same} corpus, so a
system cannot collect the one without risking the other. And it is null on both
conversational corpora, which is the setting this paper is nominally about. A reader looking
for the deployable recommendation in this paper will not find it here either.

\paragraph{Two strata carry more weight than their size supports.} The cardinality table
(Table~\ref{tab:cardinality}) rests in two places on a $23$-query stratum: \textsc{SciFact}
multi-relevant and \textsc{NFCorpus} single-relevant. One of those two is the single
confirmation of the prediction, so the one piece of evidence \emph{for} our own hypothesis
is also among the weakest in the table. We have left both rows in rather than dropping them,
and flagged them in the caption, because dropping the inconvenient-but-weak row and keeping
the convenient-but-weak one is precisely how a table acquires a thumb on the scale.

\paragraph{An experimental record can fork, and ours did.} Two programmes in this project
ran concurrently and both numbered their experiments \texttt{X100}--\texttt{X112}, so the
same identifier names different experiments depending on which record a reader consults, and
a merge of the two documentation files resolved in favour of one --- leaving a committed
documentation file that described seven completed corpora as ``not run'' while their result
files sat in the repository. We renumbered the second programme to \texttt{X200}--\texttt{X212}
and the ledger now carries both phases. We report it because it is the same failure as the
others in \S\ref{sec:protocol}, one level up: not a wrong number, but a record that
disagreed with the data on disk, and no test can catch that --- the results were correct,
reproducible and committed the whole time, and only the prose describing them was wrong.

\paragraph{Biological framing is analogy.} We use the words basin, attractor and
consolidation because they are the established vocabulary of the formalism. We make no claim
of correspondence with neural mechanism, and no result here depends on one.

\paragraph{The remaining axes have now been tested, and none of them helps.} An earlier
version of this section listed G2 and the controller as unevaluated. Both have since been
measured, on a multi-target variant of the same \textsc{LoCoMo} corpus---retrieve the full
set of annotated evidence turns for each of $1{,}977$ questions---with the conversation as
the resampling cluster. Ten clusters floor a blocked-permutation $p$ at $2/2^{10} = 0.002$,
so we give intervals and not $p$-values.

\textbf{G2 (strength-modulated basin depth) hurts under every prior we tried}---recency,
centrality, session size, turn length---and hurts monotonically in the bias strength. The
gentlest setting is indistinguishable from no bias at all ($-0.004$ \ci{-0.035}{+0.030})
and the strongest costs $-0.495$ \ci{-0.533}{-0.459} (\xid{X62}). \textbf{G3 with a
\emph{fitted} routing operator} reproduces the \xid{X18} verdict by an independent route:
diagonal routing costs $-0.029$ \ci{-0.046}{-0.013} at its mildest and dense routing
$-0.471$ \ci{-0.512}{-0.424} at its strongest, so $R{=}I$---the corner---is the best
routing we have been able to find (\xid{X63}). The lifecycle operations built on G2, decay
and consolidation, are still implemented and unevaluated; that part of the gap stands.

\paragraph{A single encoder per modality.} Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} is
encoder-independent, but the $\beta$ band is not, and we measured it on one text encoder and
one vision backbone. The claim that the band must be re-measured per encoder is supported by
two points (synthetic and \texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2}), which establishes that it varies but
not how it varies.
