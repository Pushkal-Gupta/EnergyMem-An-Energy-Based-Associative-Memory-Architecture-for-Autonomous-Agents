\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} and Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneracy}}
\label{app:proof}

Throughout, $X = [z_1\cdots z_N] \in \R^{d\times N}$ with $\|z_i\|=1$, the cue satisfies
$\|q\|=1$, and we set $b=0$, $\eta=0$, $T=1$. Write $s_i = \langle z_i,q\rangle \in [-1,1]$
for the logits, $p(\beta) = \sm(\beta s) \in \Delta^{N-1}$, and
$\xib(\beta) = X p(\beta)$ for the unnormalised state after one update.

\subsection{The unique-maximiser case}

\begin{lemma}[Softmax concentration]
\label{lem:conc}
Let $i^\star = \arg\max_i s_i$ be unique and let
$\delta \;=\; s_{i^\star} - \max_{i\neq i^\star} s_i \;>\;0$
be the top-two gap. Then
\[
1 - p_{i^\star}(\beta) \;\le\; (N-1)\,e^{-\beta\delta},
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{i\neq i^\star} p_i(\beta) \;\le\; (N-1)\,e^{-\beta\delta}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$p_{i^\star}(\beta)^{-1} = 1 + \sum_{i\neq i^\star} e^{-\beta(s_{i^\star}-s_i)}
\le 1 + (N-1)e^{-\beta\delta}$, so
$p_{i^\star} \ge (1+(N-1)e^{-\beta\delta})^{-1} \ge 1-(N-1)e^{-\beta\delta}$,
using $(1+u)^{-1}\ge 1-u$ for $u\ge 0$. The second bound is $1-p_{i^\star}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[State convergence with rate]
\label{lem:state}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:conc},
$\big\|\xib(\beta) - z_{i^\star}\big\| \;\le\; 2(N-1)\,e^{-\beta\delta}$.
Consequently $\xib(\beta)\to z_{i^\star}$ as $\beta\to\infty$, and since
$\|z_{i^\star}\|=1$ the projection $\Pi$ is continuous at the limit and
$\Pi[\xib(\beta)]\to z_{i^\star}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\varepsilon = \sum_{i\neq i^\star}p_i = 1-p_{i^\star}$. Then
$\xib - z_{i^\star} = (p_{i^\star}-1)z_{i^\star} + \sum_{i\neq i^\star}p_i z_i$, so by the
triangle inequality and $\|z_i\|=1$, $\|\xib - z_{i^\star}\| \le \varepsilon + \varepsilon
= 2\varepsilon$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:conc}. For the last statement, $\|\xib\| \ge
\|z_{i^\star}\| - 2\varepsilon = 1-2\varepsilon > 0$ for $\beta$ large, so $\Pi$ is
well defined and Lipschitz in a neighbourhood of $z_{i^\star}$.
\end{proof}

\noindent This proves the state half of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, with an explicit
$O(e^{-\beta\delta})$ rate governed by the top-two similarity gap. Note the rate depends on
$\delta$, not on $N$ except logarithmically: $\beta \ge \delta^{-1}\log\!\big((N-1)/\epsilon\big)$
suffices for $\|\xib - z_{i^\star}\|\le 2\epsilon$.

\subsection{The $k>1$ case: ranking, not state}

Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} as used in this paper is a statement about the \emph{top-$k$
set}, which requires a separate argument because the state converges to a single pattern and
the ranking is read off that state. Let $\pi_\beta$ be the ordering of $\{1..N\}$ by
$\langle z_i, \Pi[\xib(\beta)]\rangle$ descending, and let $\pi_{\cos}$ be the ordering by
$\langle z_i, q\rangle$ descending.

\begin{lemma}[Rank-1 agreement]
\label{lem:rank1}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:conc}, for $\beta$ sufficiently large
$\pi_\beta(1) = \pi_{\cos}(1) = i^\star$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:state}, $\langle z_{i^\star},\Pi[\xib]\rangle \to 1$ while for $j\neq
i^\star$, $\langle z_j,\Pi[\xib]\rangle \to \langle z_j, z_{i^\star}\rangle \le 1$ with
equality only if $z_j = z_{i^\star}$, excluded by distinctness of the patterns. Choosing
$\beta$ past the point where the $O(e^{-\beta\delta})$ perturbation is smaller than half the
gap $1-\max_{j\neq i^\star}\langle z_j,z_{i^\star}\rangle$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Ranks $2..k$ do not agree, and this is a result, not a gap]
\label{rem:shortlist}
For $k>1$ the two orderings are \emph{not} equal in the limit. Ranks $2..k$ of $\pi_\beta$
order the patterns by proximity to $z_{i^\star}$, whereas $\pi_{\cos}$ orders them by
proximity to $q$. The reduction therefore holds exactly at $k=1$ and as a statement about the
retrieved \emph{set} only when $q$ is close enough to $z_{i^\star}$ that the two
neighbourhoods coincide.

This is not a technical caveat; it is the mechanism behind the empirical shortlist
regression in \S\ref{sec:completion}. Descent improves top-1 while degrading recall@5
($0.605$ vs.\ cosine's $0.759$; both class-level, as everywhere in the image study ---
\texttt{exp\_multibeta\_scales.py} computes them that way and labels them so) precisely
because the converged state has forgotten the cue.
Our implementation of the dense baseline therefore reads the top-$k$ from $q$ directly, which
is both the standard definition and the configuration that \texttt{tests/test\_reduction.py}
verifies; a baseline reading ranks $2..k$ off the converged state would be a weaker baseline
and would flatter the method.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ties}

If the maximiser is not unique, let $\mathcal{M} = \arg\max_i s_i$ with $|\mathcal{M}| = m
\ge 2$. Then $p_i(\beta) \to 1/m$ for $i\in\mathcal{M}$ and $0$ otherwise, so
$\xib(\beta) \to \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} z_i$, the centroid of the tied patterns,
and $\Pi$ maps it to the normalised centroid provided that sum is non-zero. The retrieved
top-1 is then whichever tied pattern is nearest that centroid, which is an arbitrary
tie-break --- exactly as it is for exact cosine retrieval.

We record this case because it is the low-$\beta$ centroid-collapse failure of
\S\ref{sec:band} in miniature. At finite, small $\beta$ the softmax is flat not because of
exact ties but because $\beta\delta \ll 1$ for many pairs, and the state lands near the
centroid of a large effective set. At $\beta{=}16$ on a 768-dimensional sentence encoder the
effective set size $\exp(H(p))$ is $377$ patterns. The tie case and the collapse case are
the same phenomenon at different scales, which is why a single lower bound on $\beta$
describes both.

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneracy}}

Each $z_i$ is a fixed point of the update in the $\beta\to\infty$ limit: if
$\xib^{(t)} = z_j$ then $s = X^{\!\top}z_j$ is maximised uniquely at $j$ (patterns distinct,
unit norm), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:state} the next state is $z_j$ again. Combined with
Lemma~\ref{lem:state} applied at $t=0$, the trajectory reaches $z_{i^\star}$ after one step
and remains there, so $T>1$ is observationally equivalent to $T=1$, which is cosine
retrieval by Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}.

\paragraph{Scope of the empirical claim.} Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneracy} is exact only in
the limit. The empirical statement in \S\ref{sec:band} --- that a comparison run at high
$\beta$ compares cosine against cosine --- is therefore an \emph{approximation claim}, and we
state its evidential basis precisely rather than leaning on the theorem. On the held-out
image task at $\beta{=}512$, descent returns \emph{the same memory as cosine on
$99.4\%$ of queries} (same-class accuracy $0.4986$ against the cosine baseline's
$0.5000$). Decision agreement is the right statistic and accuracy is not: two
retrievers can score identically while disagreeing about what they return, and the
corollary is a claim about the choice. The theorem explains \emph{why} the gap closes and gives the rate
at which it closes; the measurement establishes that at the $\beta$ values this literature
actually uses, it has closed. Neither alone supports the claim.

\subsection{Numerical verification}

\texttt{tests/test\_reduction.py} instantiates Equation~\eqref{eq:update} with
$T{=}1$, $\beta{=}10^6$, $b{=}0$, $\eta{=}0$ and asserts top-1 agreement with an independent
exact-cosine implementation, in two settings. On isotropic synthetic Gaussians ($d{=}64$,
$N{=}500$) it agrees on $1000/1000$ random queries. On real sentence embeddings ($d{=}768$,
\texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2}) it agrees on $838/838$ perturbed-episode queries.

The second setting is the one that carries weight, and we separate them deliberately. Random
high-dimensional vectors are near-orthogonal, so the top-two gap $\delta$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:conc} is large and concentration is trivial. Real embeddings are anisotropic
--- unrelated pairs sit at cosine $0.258$, not $0$ --- so $\delta$ is substantially smaller
and the reduction is a materially stronger claim there. Both runs write their agreement
counts to \texttt{experiments/results/reduction\_check.json}; the test gates continuous
integration on every commit. We use
$\beta{=}10^6$ rather than a symbolic limit because that is the operating point the claim is
about; Lemma~\ref{lem:conc} shows $\beta\delta \gg \log N$ is the sufficient condition and
for $N \le 10^4$, $\delta \ge 10^{-3}$ it is met with large margin.

\section{Corpus construction}
\label{app:corpus}

The episodic corpus is generated by \texttt{src/energymem/eval/corpus.py}, deterministically
from a seed. It contains $122$ storylines drawn from $11$ topic families (property, travel,
health, gardening, vehicle, employment, study, finance, pets, home repair, social), each a
chronological sequence of $3$--$8$ episodes, interleaved in time so that the store is not
grouped by topic. Multi-target questions are attached to storylines with the gold episode
indices recorded at generation time.

Two deliberate difficulty devices:

\emph{Near-miss distractors.} Each storyline has a planted counterpart in the same topic
family that shares vocabulary but not referent --- the apartment-hunting thread and the
cottage thread both discuss commute times and deposits. These exist so that lexical overlap
is an active hazard rather than an accident.

\emph{Compositional filler.} $900$ additional episodes generated from a subject/verb/time
template grammar, which inflate the candidate set without introducing new storylines.

\paragraph{The X16 failure cases, in full.} These are the clearest evidence in the paper that
succession is not similarity, and they are real retrievals from the run, not illustrations.
Every returned episode is a reasonable answer to ``what is most similar?'' and a wrong answer
to ``what happened next?''

\section{The experiment ledger}
\label{app:ledger}

\S\ref{sec:protocol} makes claims about our own process---that errors were caught, that
results were overturned by reimplementation, that nothing was quietly rewritten. Those
claims are not checkable without the record, so the record ships with the paper.

\texttt{docs/10\_EXPERIMENT\_LEDGER.md} is append-only. Each entry carries an identifier, a
date, the question asked, the result, and what the result changed. \textbf{An entry is never
edited after its result is in.} When a later run overturns an earlier one, that is a new
entry pointing back at the old, and the old entry stays as written---so \xid{X19} still
records the $+0.185$ two-hop figure in its original wording, and \xid{X26b} is the entry
that withdraws it. A reader can therefore reconstruct not only what we believe now but what
we believed then and why we stopped.

The ledger currently runs X1--X26c across five phases: synthetic validation (X1--X6),
multi-answer readout (X7--X9), real embeddings (X10--X15), the world-model turn
(X16--X19), the mode-averaging gate (X23--X25b), and external validity (X26--X26c).

\paragraph{One gap, stated rather than papered over.} Three studies predate the ledger
convention and carry no \xid{X} identifier: the image same-class study, the anchored
\textsc{LoCoMo} sweep, and multi-scale $\beta$. They are cited in this paper by script name
instead. We have deliberately not back-numbered them, because inserting entries into an
append-only record retrospectively is precisely the manipulation the record exists to
prevent. The ledger preamble says the same. The audit that reinterpreted the first of the
three \emph{does} carry an identifier (\xid{X113}) and a pre-registration committed before
its driver existed, which is why the two can be read against each other.

\section{LoCoMo association probes}
\label{app:locomo}

\paragraph{Task.} Cue is a conversational turn; the gold target is the turn that followed it
in the same session. This is the same probe design as the episodic corpus
(Appendix~\ref{app:corpus}), applied to a benchmark we did not build.

\paragraph{Candidate set.} The \emph{full pooled bank} of all $5{,}882$ turns from all ten
conversations. A prediction must beat distractors from nine unrelated conversations as well
as from its own, which makes the task strictly harder than per-conversation retrieval and is
why absolute scores are lower here than on the episodic corpus (cosine Recall@1 $0.064$
vs.\ $0.105$).

\paragraph{Namespacing.} Dialogue identifiers are namespaced by \texttt{sample\_id}. Without
this, $5{,}882$ turns carry only $1{,}033$ distinct identifiers and a turn from an unrelated
conversation satisfies a gold identifier and scores as a hit --- the bug that invalidated an
earlier capacity sweep of ours (\S\ref{sec:semantic}).

\paragraph{Split unit.} The \emph{session} ($272$ of them across ten conversations), not the
conversation and not the turn. A session is a coherent conversational episode; transitions
from a session the model was fitted on are never evaluated. Four-fold rotation places every
session in the test set exactly once, with the validation fold disjoint from both. The world
model is fitted on ${\approx}2{,}800$ train-session transitions per fold, and both
$(k_P,\tau)$ and the readout's $(K,\beta,\alpha)$ are selected on that fold's validation
sessions.

\paragraph{Probe subsampling.} Sessions vary in length, so at most $25$ probes per session
enter the evaluation, sampled with a fixed seed. This keeps folds balanced and costs nothing
statistically: the bootstrap resamples sessions, so the interval is driven by the $272$
clusters rather than by the probe count.

\paragraph{Full results.} Table~\ref{tab:locomo-full}. Note the asymmetry that bounds the
claim: at one hop the effect is large and significant on both metrics; at two hops it is null
at Recall@1 and carries only a residual $+0.012$ at Recall@5, an order of magnitude smaller.
