\subsection{Agent memory systems: sophistication on the write path}

MemGPT frames long-term memory as an operating-system paging problem, with the model managing
transfers between a context window and external storage \citep{packer2023memgpt}. MemoryBank
adds a forgetting curve over stored summaries \citep{zhong2024memorybank}. A-MEM constructs
interlinked notes with agent-generated structure \citep{xu2025amem}. HeLa-Mem introduces
Hebbian co-activation and an episodic-to-semantic distillation stage \citep{zhu2026helamem}.
These systems differ substantially in \emph{what is written, summarised, linked and evicted},
and they are near-identical in how a memory is read: embed the query, take the top-$k$
cosine neighbours.

HippoRAG is the most prominent attempt to move past that read operator, replacing flat
similarity with personalised PageRank over a constructed knowledge graph
\citep{gutierrez2024hipporag}. Its association structure is \emph{spatial}---built from
extracted entities and relations---where ours is \emph{temporal}, learned from the order in
which the agent's own episodes arrived, with no extraction or graph-construction stage. The
two are complementary rather than competing, and we do not claim superiority over it; we
have not run the comparison.

\subsection{Associative and energy-based memory: sophistication on the read path}

The classical Hopfield network stores patterns as minima of an energy function and recalls by
descent \citep{hopfield1982}, with capacity ${\approx}0.14d$ before basins merge. Dense
associative memory raises capacity with higher-order interactions
\citep{krotov2016dense}, and the continuous modern formulation attains exponential capacity
with a one-step retrieval guarantee \citep{ramsauer2021hopfield}. Universal Hopfield Networks
place these variants in one framework of similarity, separation and projection
\citep{millidge2022universal}. Dynamic Manifold Hopfield Networks modulate attractor geometry
by context \citep{li2025dmhn}, and coupled-oscillator formulations separate stored patterns
from a learned routing layer \citep{guo2026oscillator}---the latter directly motivating our
decision to keep $R$ separate from $X$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:update}.

This literature almost exclusively evaluates on synthetic patterns or image reconstruction.
Our completion result (\S\ref{sec:completion}) is consistent with it, and
\S\ref{sec:semantic} is the finding that does not transfer: the regime these papers study is
the one in which the mechanism works, and agent memory retrieval is predominantly not that
regime.

\subsection{The equivalence, and why it has not been exploited}

\citet{ramsauer2021hopfield} prove that the modern Hopfield update is the attention
mechanism of the transformer. This is usually read as a statement about transformers. Read in
the other direction it says that a one-step, high-$\beta$ Hopfield retrieval over a memory
bank \emph{is} an attention read over that bank---and, with normalised patterns and no
strength term, is exactly cosine top-$k$. Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} is a corollary of
their result rather than a new theorem, and we claim no priority for it. What we claim is the
consequence: it is the reason the comparison between the two literatures has been circular
wherever it has been attempted, and it should be stated as the first line of any such
comparison rather than discovered in review.

\subsection{Prediction in representation space}

The joint-embedding predictive architecture proposes predicting the representation of
unobserved content rather than the content itself \citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}. Our
module $P$ (\S\ref{sec:worldmodel}) is a JEPA-shaped predictor over an episode stream, and
\S\ref{sec:composition} is, to our knowledge, the first report that the \emph{parametric}
version of this fails in the low-data regime an individual agent's history actually provides
($1.000$ on fitted storylines, $0.000$ on unseen), with a diagnosis---near-orthogonal
displacements, no global transition direction---rather than a tuning complaint.

Predictive Associative Memory is the closest prior work: it retrieves by temporal
co-occurrence using an external predictor alongside the memory \citep{dury2026pam}. Our
original claimed contribution against it was \emph{integration}---folding the predictor into
the energy functional instead of running it beside. \S\ref{sec:negatives} reports that this
does not work, at any coupling strength, and withdraws the claim. What remains distinct is
the reduction frame, the regime taxonomy, and the nonparametric form of the predictor.

\subsection{Mixtures, sampling, and statistical practice}

The rank argument for mixtures of softmaxes \citep{yang2018softmax} establishes that our
integrated multi-$\beta$ variant is not reducible to any single $\beta$; we confirm the
expressiveness empirically and find it performance-neutral (Table~\ref{tab:multibeta}).
The stochastic relaxation we specify and then decline to build on rests on Boltzmann machines
\citep{ackley1985boltzmann} and Langevin sampling \citep{welling2011sgld}. Residual peeling
is matching pursuit \citep{mallat1993matching} over the energy landscape, and repulsive
parallel descent is Stein-variational in flavour \citep{liu2016svgd}. Our interval procedure
follows the clustered bootstrap \citep{cameron2008bootstrap}; the headroom that makes any of
these measurable at all on text is a consequence of embedding anisotropy
\citep{ethayarajh2019contextual,gao2019representation}, without which, as
\S\ref{sec:setup} notes, cosine saturates and nothing can be compared.
